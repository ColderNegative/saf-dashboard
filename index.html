Method

The calculator follows the same chain as aviation_fuel_model.py: aircraft energy from the Breguet range equation, fuel cost, well-to-wake emissions, then the cost of each tonne of CO₂e avoided. It adds aircraft-specific calibration, a reserve-fuel model, live prices, blend properties checked against the fuel specification, and payload–range diagrams.

1. Blend properties (volume blending, as in ASTM D7566)

ρ_b = φ·ρ_SAF + (1 − φ)·ρ_jet (kg/m³) w = φ·ρ_SAF / ρ_b (SAF mass fraction) LHV_b = w·LHV_SAF + (1 − w)·LHV_jet (MJ/kg) e = w·LHV_SAF / LHV_b (SAF energy fraction) CI_b = e·CI_SAF + (1 − e)·89 (gCO₂e/MJ) A_b = φ·A_SAF + (1 − φ)·A_jet (aromatics, % vol)

2. Aircraft performance

R = K · (LHV / g) · ln(m_TO / m_L), K = η₀ · (L/D) BRF = K · LHV / g f = exp((R + R_allow) / BRF) − 1 m_res = m_ZF · (exp(R_res / BRF) − 1) alternate + final reserve trip = (m_ZF + m_res) · f / (1 − c·f) c = contingency fraction block = trip·(1 + c) + m_res, TOW = m_ZF + block TSFC_b / TSFC_jet = LHV_jet / LHV_b at equal η₀ and flight speed

K is solved for each aircraft so that Jet A-1 reproduces the manufacturer's quoted range at typical seating (100 kg per passenger including bags), at MTOW or full tanks. The same K is used for every drop-in fuel, so the model assumes η₀ and L/D do not change with the fuel. For the payload–range diagram the fuel load at each payload is the smaller of MTOW − m_ZF and tank volume × fuel density.

3. Cost and emissions

p_b = w·p_SAF + (1 − w)·p_jet $/t of blend cost = trip · p_b CO₂e = trip · LHV · CI well-to-wake CO₂_ETS = trip · (1 − w) · 3.16 eligible SAF counts as zero ΔC = cost_blend − cost_jet ΔE = CO₂e_jet − CO₂e_blend abatement = ΔC / ΔE $/tCO₂e net = ΔC − carbon price · ΔCO₂_ETS (or · ΔE on a life-cycle basis)

The abatement cost equals the life-cycle carbon price at which the blend breaks even. The break-even SAF and jet prices solve net = 0 for p_SAF and p_jet. EU ETS zero-rating is applied only where the fuel's life-cycle saving is at least 65% (70% for e-fuels), approximating the RED II/III threshold; RED's own calculation excludes land-use change and uses a 94 gCO₂e/MJ comparator, so eligibility for a real batch can differ. Fuels below the threshold are charged like fossil kerosene.

4. Assumptions and limits

  • Drop-in fuels change only LHV and density in the performance model. Engine efficiency, L/D and OEW are unchanged.
  • Reserves default to 5% contingency plus alternate and final reserve equivalent to 600 km of cruise, and 200 km of equivalent cruise for taxi, climb and descent. All editable under Advanced assumptions.
  • Airbus does not publish OEW; the values used are typical figures.
  • Life-cycle values are ICAO CORSIA defaults (core LCA + induced land-use change). Certified plants can claim lower actual values.
  • PtL e-SPK has no CORSIA default; its value is built up from the electricity intensity (0.70 kWh per MJ of fuel) plus 2 gCO₂e/MJ for plant and transport.
  • Density and aromatics blend linearly by volume. Freezing point, viscosity and thermal stability are not modelled.
  • Non-CO₂ effects are not included. SAF blends have fewer aromatics, which cuts soot and contrail ice crystals; this benefit is not credited here.
  • Mandate shares (ReFuelEU Aviation, UK SAF Mandate) are defined in the regulations' own terms; the blend chips apply them as volume shares for illustration.
  • Jet prices convert between units at IATA's factor of 7.897 barrels per tonne. Currencies convert at the ECB reference rate in the feed. EASA euro values convert at 1.161 $/€, the rate implied by EASA's 2025 dollar figures.

5. Live price feed

The page reads data/feed.json from this site. A GitHub Action rewrites it on weekday mornings from the IATA Jet Fuel Price Monitor, EIA spot prices via FRED and ECB reference rates, and tries a public EU ETS quote. The SAF price and, if the automatic quote fails, the carbon price come from data/manual.json, which you edit by hand. If the file can't be loaded, the page uses its built-in snapshot and says so at the top.
